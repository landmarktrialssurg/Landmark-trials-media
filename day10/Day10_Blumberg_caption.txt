The man who found the virus behind hepatitis B was not looking for a virus.

Baruch "Barry" Blumberg (1925-2011) was studying inherited variation in blood proteins. In the early 1960s, serum from a haemophilia patient in New York reacted with serum from an Aboriginal Australian: the "Australia antigen".

By 1967 his group showed it was the surface antigen of hepatitis B, today's HBsAg. With Irving Millman at Fox Chase in Philadelphia he built a blood test (blood banks screened donations from 1971) and proposed a vaccine made from purified surface antigen. A plasma-derived vaccine reached the US market in 1982.

Because hepatitis B causes liver cancer, it is often called the first vaccine to prevent a cancer. Nobel Prize 1976, shared with Carleton Gajdusek.

Every HBsAg in your preoperative workup traces back to him.
